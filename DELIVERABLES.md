## Time-based rules
- Weekend (+5%): `is_weekend == 1` → multiply price by 1.05.
- Festival (+10%): `Season == 'Festival'` → multiply price by 1.10.
- Month-end (+2%): `DAY >= 25` → multiply price by 1.02.
- Low demand discount (-5%): `Conversion Rate % < 1` → multiply price by 0.95.

## Inventory-based rules
- Low stock (<20) → increase price by 7% (multiply by 1.07).
- Moderate stock (20–60) → small increase 3% (multiply by 1.03).
- Overstock (>200) → discount 10% (multiply by 0.90).
- Inventory ratio scarcity: if `inventory_ratio < 0.6` → multiply price by 1.05.
- Inventory ratio overstock: if `inventory_ratio > 1.2` → multiply price by 0.93.

(Inventory ratio is computed as `Stock Level / (Units Sold + 1e-6)` as a proxy for stock pressure.)

## Demand response / Elasticity handling
- If `elasticity` is available per row, it is used to estimate change in units.
- Fallback elasticity used where missing: **-0.5** (moderately elastic).
- Elasticity values clipped to `[-5, 0.5]` to avoid extreme responses.
- Estimated percent change in units = `elasticity_filled * pct_price_change`.
- Estimated percent change is clipped to `[-70%, +200%]` to prevent unrealistic swings.
- Expected units = `Units Sold * (1 + pct_units_change_est)`, rounded to integer.

## Brief strategy explanation
The rule engine applies realistic, interpretable rules that combine time signals (weekend, festival, month-end) with inventory signals (stock level and inventory ratio). Prices are adjusted conservatively and then demand response is estimated via observed or fallback elasticities so revenue is computed with expected units (not assuming fixed units). This produces a more realistic estimation of uplifts.
